Us vs AI · for the tutor

Table packs

Print pages 2 to 6 once for each table. Colour if you can. Cut along the dashed lines. Put each table's pack in an envelope so it can be opened on cue.

Learners stand at the whiteboard tables and write on the table itself, around the cards and sheets. Nothing needs writing on the paper, so packs can be reused.

Pack 1 · AI or not AI? (steps 4 and 5)

Tables sort the 16 cards under the three headings and write one reason next to any card they argued about. The point is the argument, not a perfect score.

Uses AIWhy
Face unlock, "watch next" suggestions, spam filter, voice assistant, fraud alert, translation app, chatbotEach one learned from huge numbers of examples. Nobody wrote a rule for every case.
Predictive textA small cousin of a chatbot: it guesses your next word. Come back to this card in step 7.
Does not use AIWhy
Calculator, microwave timer, light switch, alarm clock, word search (Ctrl+F)They follow fixed rules a person wrote. Same input, same answer, every time.
It dependsWhy
Sat-navFinding the route follows rules. Guessing the traffic and your arrival time uses AI.
Self-checkoutScanning a barcode is not AI. A camera that recognises loose fruit is.
Automatic doorUsually just a sensor. Praise anyone who argues it well either way.

Pack 2 · Case file (step 11)

A good answer has the full chain: what it saw (watch adverts, nearly all at ten past ten), the pattern (watch means 10:10), what it made (10:10 again, whatever was asked). Stretch answers might mention AI pictures of "a nurse" or "a boss" copying who appears most in its pictures.

Pack 3 · Fix the instruction (steps 12 and 13)

Each table rewrites its instruction on the table. Then tables swap: the visiting table reads the black card and plays a very literal AI, looking for any way to get it wrong. The home table fixes each gap they find.

Case file
Pack 2 · Why did it do that?

The watch that is always ten past ten

You drew half past three with no trouble. An AI picture tool was asked for the same thing. Work out what went wrong. Write your answer on the table.

EVIDENCE A · WHAT WE ASKED FOR
"A watch showing half past three"
EVIDENCE B · WHAT IT DREW
Ten past ten. Again.
EVIDENCE C · WHERE IT LEARNED
Millions of pictures from the internet

It never saw a real watch. It only saw pictures of them.

EVIDENCE D · A CLUE
Look at any watch advert

Shops set the hands to ten past ten. It frames the logo and looks like a smile.

1. What did the AI see?
2. What pattern did it learn?
3. So why could it not draw half past three?
STRETCH

Where else could copying a pattern be unfair or harmful? Think about pictures of people, jobs or places.
