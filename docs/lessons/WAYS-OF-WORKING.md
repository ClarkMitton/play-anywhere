# Good ways of working

What we have learned makes a lesson work well in the Immersive Learning Room.
Each point came from building or running a real lesson. The story behind it is
in [WWW.md](WWW.md). Add to this whenever something proves itself.

## Videos

- **Let the video move the lesson on.** When a video on the big screen finishes, the lesson goes to the next step by itself after a short pause. Nobody has to find Next over YouTube's end screen. So put the step you want straight after the video directly after it.
- **Follow every video with something to do.** Clip, then the one idea it shows, then an activity that uses it. Never two clips back to back.
- **Give the touch screens a job during a video.** Show the question to think about ("What goes wrong, and why?"), not a second copy of the clip.
- **Trim a long video to the part that matters.** Add `&end=460` to a YouTube link to stop it at 7 minutes 40 seconds. The lesson then moves on from there.
- **Use a video file for anything that must loop.** A Canva video cannot loop or start itself as an embed. Download it from Canva as an MP4 and add it as "Video file (loops)".
- **Check every link is a different video** before building around them.

## Screens

- **Both touch screens show the same activity.** Half the class is standing at each one.
- **Put the whiteboard on the big screen too**, or the drawings learners send have nowhere to appear.
- **Hotspot activities are set up once, on any screen.** The other two follow.
- **Keep on-screen text short.** It is read from across a room. The detail belongs in what the tutor says.
- **Give slides a title** when the text needs context. It sits in a box at the top.

## Activities

- **Teach before you test.** A question should follow the slide or clip that answers it.
- **Pick the tool for the job.** Word cloud for many short answers. Shared board when the exact wording or capital letters matter. Vote for opinions. Question round for right answers. Buzzer for fast recall of things already taught.
- **Ask for real opinions as part of the lesson.** Votes, word clouds and shared boards save what learners say, so feedback comes from an activity they enjoyed, not a form at the end.
- **Start and end with the same confidence question** so the class sees its own change.
- **Make practice get harder,** and let learners mark their own work straight away.

## Other people's material

- **Keep a teacher's slides recognisably theirs:** their order, their words, their pictures.
- **Describe every embedded page.** The lesson plan cannot see inside a Canva or a Wordwall, so write one line saying what it shows.
- **Paste the address, not the share snippet,** when embedding. If a site gives you a block of code, the address inside it is what is needed.
- **Tell the teacher what you changed,** even a corrected spelling.

## Before it is taught

- **Send the lesson card, not a long plan.** One page, with a button into the preview.
- **Click through the preview yourself.** It shows all three screens with the notes for each step, and needs no sign-in.
- **Watch every video to the end.** Especially clips from comedy or social media channels.
- **Check anything that could have changed,** such as what an AI tool does today, or your course's rules.
