# Good ways of working

What we have learned makes a lesson work well in the Immersive Learning Room.
Each point came from building or running a real lesson. The story behind it is
in [WWW.md](WWW.md). Add to this whenever something proves itself.

The room is about new approaches to delivery and learning. A lesson here is as
much for the member of staff running it as for the learners in it.

## Before building: the brief

Get these agreed first. Building without them is what produced a quiz chain.

- **Length, level and a typical group size.**
- **What learners already know.**
- **The one idea the session is about.** If you cannot say it in a sentence, the session does not know what it is about yet.
- **The learning objectives, chosen by the person who asked for the lesson.** Offer a set to pick from.
- **What learners will make or do by the end** that proves the last objective.
- **The kit in the room:** whiteboard tables, pens, printing, anything else.
- **What must be kept** from any source material, and what can go.
- **What feedback should be collected** from learners along the way.

## The standard every lesson must meet

- **One thread.** Every clip, slide and task serves the one idea. If a video does not, drop it, however good it is.
- **Objectives climb Bloom's taxonomy,** with at least one at analyse or above. Identify, explain, analyse, create is a good ladder.
- **LEAD, properly.** Launch is a hook that opens a question. Establish builds understanding. Apply uses the new learning. Demonstrate is learners proving it, with a product tested against criteria, not a recall quiz.
- **At least two table tasks,** and at least a third of the time spent at the tables.
- **Everyone has a job.** Two touch screens cannot occupy a whole class. Table tasks can.
- **No activity twice in a row.** Mix watching, moving, writing, tapping, talking, making and judging.
- **Mix the question types.** True or false for pace. Best answer, where two options look right, for debate. Odd one out. Never two rounds of the same shape.
- **Do it before being told.** Learners predict, act out or attempt the task first, then see the explanation.
- **A stretch on every table task** for the groups who finish first or are working at a higher level.
- **Close the loop.** The question opened in Launch is answered in the last step.
- **Inclusive by design.** Pictures, names and examples reflect the college's learners, and nobody is left out by an activity that assumes money, a device, or a home set-up. See "Slides" for pictures.
- **A movement plan.** The whole class changes place as few times as possible, and every move is worth it. See "Where are the learners?" below.

## The design question

Ask this for every idea in the lesson:

> What tools have I got, and what is the most exciting way to get this across?

The tools: video, Canva slide, picture, vote, word cloud, shared board, question
round, buzzer quiz, whiteboard with send to big screen, wheel spinner, countdown,
rotation timer, hotspot picture, printed packs on the tables, Canva code activity.

## Where are the learners?

Learners are standing, at the tables or at the touch screens. Every step happens
somewhere, and getting sixteen people from one place to the other costs a minute
and breaks concentration. So plan the room, not only the content.

- **Write down where learners are for every step** before building. If the answer keeps changing, the order is wrong.
- **Ask of every move: is it worth it?** Walking the whole class to the touch screens for one vote or three taps is not.
- **Group the screen steps together.** One trip to the touch screens should cover several things: questions, a vote, a word cloud, the whiteboard. Then one trip back.
- **Aim for two whole-class moves in 45 minutes.** Tables, then screens, then tables.
- **Send a runner, not the room.** When only one answer per table is needed, one person goes to the screen.
- **Do quick checks where they stand.** Hands up, thumbs, or writing T or F on the table costs nothing. Save the touch screens for things worth saving or showing.
- **Slides and videos need no move.** Everyone can see the big screen from anywhere.
- **Tell the tutor.** Each step's note starts with where learners are, and says clearly when they move.

## Table tasks

- **Learners stand at the whiteboard tables and write on the table,** around the printed sheet. Nothing is written on the paper, so packs can be reused.
- **Print one pack per table, in an envelope,** opened on cue. Design it so it works with any number of tables.
- **Make the printouts feel like something:** a case file with evidence, cards to sort, a role card. Not a worksheet.
- **Give the tutor an answer sheet** as page one of the pack.
- **Use the rotation timer for two rounds:** do the task, then move and check or challenge another table's work.
- **Put the brief and the success criteria on the big screen** while tables work.
- **Capture the product at the end** on a shared board, so there is evidence of the learning.

## Slides

- **Make real slides in Canva** for the teaching points, so the lesson does not look like an app.
- **Brief Canva's AI precisely:** who it is for, that it is read from across a room, very few words, the exact wording per slide, the style wanted and the style to avoid, what each picture must show, and who should be in it.
- **Export the slides as pictures** and add them as picture slides. An embedded Canva loads slowly and shows Canva's own toolbar.
- **Make the people in the pictures look like the people in the room.** Across a deck, show a real mix: women and men, different ethnicities, visible faith dress such as a hijab or turban, disabled people, and a range of ages and body types. AI picture tools default to the same face again and again, so ask for the mix in the brief, slide by slide. Do not leave it to chance.
- **Avoid stereotypes in who does what.** Vary who is shown as the expert, the one using the technology, the one in charge. Show disabled people as ordinary participants, not as the subject of the picture.
- **Review the finished deck as a set,** not slide by slide. Count who appears. If one group dominates or is missing, regenerate before it goes in a lesson.
- **Check every AI picture for garbled words.** They are common. Fix them, or use them as a teaching point if the lesson is about AI.
- **Keep on-screen text short.** The detail belongs in what the tutor says.

## Videos

- **Videos start conversations.** Follow each one with a talk slide carrying one open question, never straight into a quiz.
- **The lesson moves on by itself when a video ends,** so the step after it must be the one you want next.
- **Give the touch screens a job during a video.** Show the question to think about, not a second copy of the clip.
- **Trim a long video to the part that matters.** Add `&end=460` to a YouTube link to stop it at 7 minutes 40 seconds.
- **Use a video file for anything that must loop.** A Canva video cannot loop or start itself as an embed. Download it as an MP4 and add it as "Video file (loops)".
- **Check every link is a different video** before building around them.

## Screens

- **Both touch screens show the same activity.** Half the class is standing at each one.
- **Put the whiteboard on the big screen too,** or the drawings learners send have nowhere to appear.
- **Hotspot activities are set up once, on any screen.** The other two follow.
- **Give text slides a title** when the text needs context. It sits in a box at the top.

## Activities

- **Teach before you test.** A question should follow the slide or task that answers it.
- **Pick the tool for the job.** Word cloud for many short answers. Shared board when the exact wording matters. Vote for opinions. Question round for right answers. Buzzer for fast recall of things already taught.
- **Use the word cloud to act things out.** "Finish this sentence" shows how a chatbot works better than any slide.
- **Ask for real opinions as part of the lesson.** Votes, word clouds and shared boards save what learners say.

## For the staff member

- **Name the technique in the tutor note** for each step: "predict before reveal", "best-answer question", "create, then test each other's work". The tutor leaves with approaches they can reuse.
- **Say what a good answer looks like,** not only what to say.

## Other people's material

- **Keep a teacher's slides recognisably theirs:** their order, their words, their pictures.
- **Describe every embedded page.** The lesson plan cannot see inside a Canva or a Wordwall, so write one line saying what it shows.
- **Paste the address, not the share snippet,** when embedding.
- **Tell the teacher what you changed,** even a corrected spelling.

## Before it is taught

- **Send the lesson card, not a long plan.** One page, with a button into the preview.
- **Click through the preview yourself.** It shows all three screens with the notes for each step, and needs no sign-in.
- **Print and cut the table packs.**
- **Watch every video to the end.**
- **Check anything that could have changed,** such as what an AI tool does today, or your course's rules.
