```
Build a single-page interactive activity for staff training, used on tablets and on large portrait touch screens. British English. No em dashes. No sound effects. No timer. No login.

TITLE: Fix the sheet
SUBTITLE: Same task, a better-supported route. Make this sheet work for Kai, Amira and Ryan without making it easier.

LAYOUT: the worksheet on one side (or on top on a tall screen), and a panel of change cards on the other side (or below). A counter: "4 of 7 changes made".

THE WORKSHEET, as it starts (show it exactly like this, in a dense serif font, long lines, no spacing, like a real tired handout):
Unit 3: Risk Assessment
Read the following information carefully and then complete all of the tasks below. A hazard is anything with the potential to cause harm, and risk is a combination of the likelihood that harm will occur and the severity of that harm, taking into account the number of people who may be exposed. Employers have a duty under the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 to assess risks and to implement suitable and sufficient control measures, following the hierarchy of control: elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls and personal protective equipment. You will need to apply this knowledge to your own workplace.
1. Identify five hazards in your workplace.
2. Copy the risk matrix from the board into your book.
3. Rate the likelihood and severity of each hazard using the matrix.
4. For each hazard, propose a control measure and justify why it reduces the risk.
5. Write a 300 word summary of your findings using the correct terminology.
Don't overthink it. Hand in at the end of the lesson.

NINE CHANGE CARDS (shuffled). Tapping a card applies it to the worksheet with a short, gentle animation, and the card shows a coloured tag: H = Hide the pile, N = No surprises, L = Lower the language. Applied cards can be tapped again to undo.

The seven good changes:
1. "Put the plan at the top" (N). Adds a dark teal banner at the top: "Today: 4 steps · 50 minutes · Finished = five hazards rated, each with a control measure and your reason, and a summary". Removes "Don't overthink it."
2. "One step at a time" (H). Shows only task 1 in a bordered box headed "STEP 1 OF 4", with the other tasks folded under a single line "Next: rate each hazard, propose a control measure, summarise".
3. "Add tick boxes" (H). Task 1 becomes five tick boxes: Hazard 1 to Hazard 5.
4. "Teach the key words first" (L). Replaces the long paragraph with a KEY WORDS box: "Hazard: something that could cause harm. Risk: how likely the harm is, and how bad. Control measure: what you do to make it safer."
5. "Put the matrix on the sheet" (L). Replaces "Copy the risk matrix from the board into your book" with a small 3 by 3 grid on the sheet and the line "The matrix is on this sheet".
6. "Add Listen" (L). Adds a button "Listen to this sheet" that reads the worksheet aloud using the browser's speech voice (British English if available).
7. "Offer a choice: write or record" (L). The summary task becomes "Summarise your findings: write 300 words, or record it."

When "Teach the key words first" and "One step at a time" are both applied, also switch the worksheet to a clear sans-serif font with more spacing.

The two traps. Tapping one does NOT change the sheet. Instead show an orange warning box for a few seconds, and mark the card with a cross:
8. "Cut it to three hazards" → "That changes the task, not the route. Check 6: the thinking must be as hard as it was before."
9. "Drop the 'justify why' part" → "That lowers the thinking, not the language. Keep the justification: support the route to it instead."

WHEN ALL SEVEN GOOD CHANGES ARE MADE: show a calm message: "Done. Same task, a better-supported route. What did not change? The thinking." and a "Show before and after" button that shows the original sheet and the finished sheet side by side. Also show "Traps avoided: 2 of 2" (or 1 of 2, 0 of 2).

A "Start again" button resets everything.

LOOK AND ACCESSIBILITY:
- Match the "Sort the toolkit" activity: cream background (#F3EBDD), deep teal (#1F5C57) for headings, banners and tag H/N/L chips, dark navy text (#1D2B36), one orange accent (#E07A3F) for warnings and buttons.
- Change cards are large (at least 56px high), rounded, with clear borders and large text (at least 20px).
- The adapted worksheet must look clearly better than the original: sans-serif, short lines, generous spacing, high contrast.
- No flashing, nothing on a timer. Works with a finger on a tablet and on a 1080 by 1920 portrait screen without scrolling.
```
